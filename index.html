Before anything else

A birthday wish.

Happy eighteenth, Helena.

I want to start here, before anything clever or cute, with the plainest and truest thing I have: happy birthday. Not in the polite, quick way you say it to someone you kind of know. In the real way. In the way that means I've been thinking about you, and about this day, for longer than I probably should admit.

Turning eighteen is a strange, lovely, enormous thing. You are at the exact point where the world starts asking you to become yourself more fully and I hope this year gives you the room for that. I hope it gives you small good mornings and long good nights. I hope it gives you a few people who see you clearly and stay. I hope it gives you the kind of confidence that isn't loud, just steady.

I hope it gives you yourself, a little more each month. The version of you that you've been quietly becoming for years. I hope you get to look at her sometime and think, oh. There she is.

And selfishly, because I'm allowed one selfish line; I hope I get to be somewhere in this year for you. In whatever way you'd want. A late-night call. A stupid joke at the right moment. A message that finds you on a day you needed it. I'm not asking for anything today. I'm just saying: I'd like to be around for it. And I’m just genuinely glad I get to be around for it.

So. Happy birthday. I mean it. Every word.

— Cee
And now the thing I've been meaning to say

Your voice.

I will not make up a version of you that isn't real. But your voice on calls stays with me in a way I didn't expect. It has a texture to it; warm, unhurried, a little bit musical in a way that isn't trying to be. The first time we spoke, I remember noticing it before anything else. Not in a way I could have explained then. Just: nobody else sounds like that. I could listen to this voice all day.

And the thing is, it stayed with me. On quiet days. On long days. On days when I didn't realise I was waiting to hear it. There's a specific kind of calm in the way you talk. Like someone who isn't performing. Like someone who is actually there. I don't take that for granted.

I think a lot about that n ur laugh. Not the polite one, the real one, the one that catches you off guard and makes you say "wait, say that again." The one that makes me stop what I'm doing and sit there for a second, smiling at a screen in a room by myself, a whole ocean away from you.

What I mean is simple: I love hearing your voice. I wish we'd talk way more. It's the kind of thing I'd never get tired of, and I don't think I'm exaggerating when I say that.

This little wave above isn't your voice, it's mine, imagining yours. Think of it as a portrait, drawn in sound, of the first thing I ever noticed about you.

And Helena, I mean this seriously. I hope you never feel like you have to make your voice smaller. It's beautiful exactly as it is.

Some things you don't get used to. Your voice is one of them. — Cee
Nigeria · Indonesia

A thread across the world.

Lagos to Indonesia is somewhere around eleven thousand kilometres; an ocean, a desert, and a whole lot of sky in between. Which is a very long way to say: I wish I could hand nd tell you this in person.

I think about that more than I probably let on. Not in a way that makes me sad, in a way that makes me want to be deliberate about it. If I could, I'd be the person who shows up on December 1st with something stupid and sweet, watches you open it, and then hears that laugh in real life instead of through a screen. Since I can't, this is what I've got. A glowing line, stretched from my side of the world to yours.

The distance is real. I'm not going to pretend otherwise, and I'm not going to pretend it doesn't matter. But it isn't the whole story. The whole story is that I met you, and I'm glad I did, and I want to keep closing that gap.

So here's what I actually mean: the distance is real, but it isn't the point. The point is that I'm on your side of this. Yours on the map, mine in the way I show up.

A letter for eighteen.

Dear Helena,
December 1st
from Cee

You told me once, half joking, half not; not to forget to wish you today. I want you to know there was never any version of this day where I forgot. I've been thinking about what to give you for days. I can't fly to Indonesia. I can't show up at your door with flowers. So I did the only thing I actually know how to do: I made you something.

I want to know you, Helena. Not the version of you that shows up for a while and then logs off. I mean the actual you. The one with the private favourite songs and the opinions about things that don't matter and the feelings about things that do. The one who sends me pics of her time to time. The one who's a little different on quiet days. I want to know what makes you laugh until you can't breathe, and what you're scared of, and what you were like at twelve, and what a normal Tuesday feels like in your life. I want to know the small stuff. The small stuff is the whole thing.

Happy eighteenth, Helena. I hope this year is gentle with you. I hope it gives you the good kind of surprises. I hope you get to be exactly who you are, out loud, without apology. And I hope that I get to be part of the year, in whatever way you'll have me.

One more thing, and then I'll stop. You sent me a photo of you once. I remember exactly where I was when I opened it. I remember thinking oh damn She's actually beautiful. Not just pretty. Warm. Real. The kind of beautiful that has nothing to do with angles. I didn't say it well then because I didn't want to make it weird. So I'm saying it now, an entire birthday later: you're lovely. I noticed. I remember.

Here's to eighteen. Here's to you. Here's to finding out what happens next.

— Cee
